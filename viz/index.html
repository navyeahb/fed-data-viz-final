03 — PEAK DOMINANCE

Nobody has EVER had a peak like this

Djokovic's whole thing is accumulation — more Slams, more weeks at No. 1, still racking it up in his late 30s (annoying, honestly). But Federer's most ridiculous records aren't about totals at all. They're about a peak nobody else has ever touched, before or since.

And to be fair to Djokovic — his longest match win streak (43, ending at the 2011 French Open) is actually longer than Federer's (41). So no, peak dominance isn't a total sweep either. How I'm calculating all of this
Ace rate
aces ÷ serve points — both serves combined.
Net point frequency
net points played ÷ (serve points + return points) — how often a point ended with the player at net, as a share of every point they played.
Winner : unforced error ratio
winners ÷ unforced errors. Above 1.0 means more clean winners than self-inflicted errors.
Return points won
return points won ÷ total return points.
1st serve points won
first-serve points won ÷ first serves in.

Quick disclaimer: these are career totals across every match I have data for (filtered by whatever surface you've picked above), not per-match averages — so a stat naturally weights toward however many points that player actually played.

05 — EVEN IN DEFEAT

The better shot-maker, even when he lost

Zooming into just their 47 head-to-heads: Federer out-served and out-hit Djokovic on a per-point basis, even though he lost the rivalry overall. Make it make sense.

The Verdict

The scoreboard says Djokovic. The tennis says Federer.

Look, Djokovic won more of their matches, and he's built the longer, more decorated career off elite returning and honestly remarkable consistency. I'm not going to pretend otherwise. But shot for shot, Federer played the harder, more varied, more attacking brand of tennis — and the data backs that up even in the matches he lost. That's my case, and I'm sticking to it.

How I'm scoring Consistency, Peak, Head-to-Head, and Longevity
Consistency computed from data
Average, across three metrics, of Federer ÷ (Federer + Djokovic) — and for the two "lower is better" stats, whoever has the lower rate gets the bigger share:
  • 1st serve in %: Djokovic actually leads this one
  • Double fault rate (lower is better): Federer leads
  • Unforced error rate (lower is better): Djokovic leads
Averaging those three gives …. I picked these three on purpose — not ace rate or net frequency, which are Federer's best stats basically by definition. Djokovic wins 2 of 3 here, which is why this lands close to even instead of conveniently favoring Federer.
Head-to-Head computed from data
Federer's wins ÷ total charted meetings across their 47 meetings. Right now that's ….
Peak from the ATP record book
Average of three pure streak comparisons — concentrated dominance, not career totals — each scored as Federer ÷ (Federer + Djokovic):
  • Longest streak at world No. 1: Federer 237 weeks vs Djokovic 122 weeks
  • Consecutive Grand Slam semifinals: Federer 23 vs Djokovic 14
  • Longest match win streak: Federer 41 vs Djokovic 43 (Djokovic's is actually longer — I'm including it anyway)
Averaging those three gives …. Choosing which records count toward "peak" is still a judgment call on my part — the arithmetic on top of that choice isn't.
Longevity from the ATP record book
Average of two career-length comparisons, each scored as Federer ÷ (Federer + Djokovic):
  • Total weeks at world No. 1: Federer 310 vs Djokovic 428 (an all-time record, and still rising)
  • Years as a touring pro: Federer 24 (1998–2022) vs Djokovic 23 and counting (turned pro 2003, still going)
